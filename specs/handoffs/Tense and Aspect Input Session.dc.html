Tense and aspect.
Tense marks when. Aspect marks how the speaker views the action — complete, ongoing, or connected to another point in time. Most language-analysis errors trace back to confusing the two.
Running this session
Don't lead with terminology. Start from a situation that needs a particular aspect, and let trainees notice why the "obvious" tense doesn't fit — the same principle as guided discovery, applied to grammar most trainees think they already know.
Lead-in · 2 minutes
"I've read three books this month" and "I've been reading a book about Istanbul all month." Both present perfect. What's different, and it isn't tense?

Tense and aspect are not the same thing
"Present perfect" is a tense-and-aspect label, not a single tense — present tense, perfect aspect. Trainees who conflate the two end up teaching form without meaning.
Meaning before form, every time
Establish the situation that needs continuous or perfect aspect before naming or drilling the structure — the same guided-discovery principle, applied to grammar most trainees assume they already know.
Future is a modal, not a tense in English
English has no future tense inflection — "will" is a modal verb. "Future continuous" is really modal + continuous aspect. Worth flagging so trainees don't teach three "future tenses" as if they were parallel to past and present.
