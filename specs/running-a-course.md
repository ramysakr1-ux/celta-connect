# Running a course on Connect

For centre directors, administrators and tutors. Everything you need to run a CELTA course start to finish, and the reasons behind the parts that look opinionated.

If you only read one page, read **The five things that matter** at the end.

---

## Before you start

### What Connect is, and is not

Connect is where a course is **run**. It holds the timetable, the teaching practice, the assignments, the assessment and the people, from application to certificate.

It is **not** where your course is stored. At the end of every course, everything is exported as PDFs to your own Google Drive — portfolios, CELTA 5 records, reports, certificates — and those files are yours. If you stopped using Connect tomorrow, every course you have ever run is still readable on your Drive.

It is also **not** connected to Cambridge. Entry forms and grades go through Appian as they always have. Connect holds your course number and records when each step happened, so anyone can answer "where are we" without asking.

### Setting up your centre — once

Four steps, done once when you join:

1. **Centre profile** — name, address, logo. The logo appears on every document you generate. Cambridge-facing documents carry your logo, never ours.
2. **Connect your Drive** — this is where courses export at the end.
3. **Import your documents** — your assignment briefs and cover sheets. Connect reads them and turns their criteria into fields candidates fill in. After the import, the Word files are finished; your wording lives on as data.
4. **Add your tutors** — with their Cambridge verification status.

After this you have a **shell**: your resource hub, your TP points library, your assignment criteria, your time bands, your tutors. Every future course starts from a copy of it. You build it once.

## Running a course

### The order matters

The timetable is the clock. Everything else reads from it — TP dates, assignment deadlines, rotation rounds, what a candidate sees on their home screen. So:

1. Create the course: name, dates, hours, tutors.
2. Add candidates, or send them a self-join link.
3. Split them into TP groups.
4. **Generate and lock the timetable.**
5. Set the rotation.
6. Assign and release TP points.
7. Register volunteer students.
8. Send candidate invitations.

Steps 5, 6 and 7 will not let you proceed before step 4. That is deliberate — a rotation built on an unlocked timetable breaks the moment a date moves.

### Applications

Your website links to your own application page. An applicant fills it in with no account, chooses which course they want, and appears in your pipeline.

Four stages: **applied → task returned → interviewed → offered**. Rejections sit to one side, kept for the assessor.

Things worth knowing:

- **All applicants must be interviewed** and **must submit written tasks before acceptance** — Cambridge requires both. Connect will not let you make an offer without them.
- **Selection must be done by a verified course tutor** or a nominated person.
- **Rejections require a written reason.** Connect will not send one without it. This is not bureaucracy: a rejection that explains itself is the difference between someone who reapplies and someone who tells others not to bother.
- **An apology for a full course sends itself** on the date you promised. A rejection on the merits never does.
- If the course fills, applicants go on a waiting list with a date by which they will hear either way. When a place frees, Connect tells you who is next.

### The teaching practice loop

This repeats through the course:

**TP points released → candidate plans → teaches → self-evaluates → tutor feedback → starred points carry into the next plan.**

The last step is the one that makes it a loop. Action points you star in feedback appear automatically as personal aims in that candidate's next lesson plan. Nobody copies anything across.

**Rotation** splits each group of six into halves of three, teaching on alternate days. Set the base order once; every round derives from it, so nobody is always first. If a candidate withdraws, their slot empties and nobody else moves.

**Peer observation**: the five who are not teaching write two short notes during each lesson. At the feedback slot everything unlocks at once — the three who taught write their self-evaluations, the three who did not read the notes and agree the group feedback, then oral feedback. Nobody sits in a room while others discuss them.

### Assignments

Four assignments, one resubmission each, three of four must pass to be eligible for a Pass. Passing on resubmission does not affect the certificate grade — tell candidates this, because they will assume otherwise.

- **Withdrawing a submission before a tutor opens it never uses up the resubmission.** Nor does a tutor returning work unmarked because it was the wrong file or incomplete. Only "resubmission needed" as a marking outcome does.
- Marking is **Met / Not met per criterion**, per round, as your cover sheets do.
- A **minimum of two tutors** must be involved in marking, and a proportion of each assignment must be double-marked: 3 of each for up to 9 candidates, 4 for up to 16, 5 for up to 24. **The sample must include any fails.** Connect tracks this and produces the record your assessor may ask for.
- Assignment briefs carry a **hidden required word** in each section. A candidate who did not write their own work will not include it. Connect checks automatically and tells the tutor — it is never an accusation and never an automatic fail.

### Tutorials and warnings

Three stages, and Stage 3 is the one with teeth.

- **Stage 1** — all candidates, tutorial optional.
- **Stage 2** — all candidates, **tutorial required**, triggered by hours of assessed teaching rather than by the calendar.
- **Stage 3** — required in the final third for anyone who was not to standard at Stage 2, was at or above standard but is not progressing, or was indicated Pass B or A and has not maintained it. Connect flags all four cases.

A **Fail letter** must be issued with **at least two lessons left to teach**, so the candidate can respond. Connect warns you when a candidate at risk drops below two remaining lessons with no letter sent.

### Grades

Provisional grades are tutor-only and never visible to a candidate.

A grade written with a slash — Fail/Pass, Pass/Pass B, Pass B/Pass A — means undecided, and **requires written justification** before the grade can be finalised. Definite grades require nothing written.

Then: MCT enters provisionals in Appian → sends the course number to the assessor → grade meeting → final report to the assessor → **the assessor enters the final grades**. Connect produces the report and records the dates.

### The assessor visit

Connect assembles the pack and gives the assessor a link. No account, no password.

The pack includes portfolios, candidate descriptions, the timetable, the TP schedule for the day, assignment titles, application files (including rejected applicants), lesson plans for that day, volunteer attendance registers, the double-marking record, a sample end-of-course report, and the previous assessor's report.

**Portfolio selection**: a minimum of four, and it must include everyone the assessor observes teaching, everyone provisionally graded Fail or potential Fail, and every withdrawn candidate. Pass A and potential A are recommended. Connect proposes the mandatory set; you complete it.

If you have a **trainer-in-training** on the external scheme, book the assessor for an **extra day**.

### Closing the course

One action does three things:

1. **Exports** everything to your Drive as PDFs — a folder per candidate, plus the course-level documents.
2. **Erases** candidate accounts, submissions, feedback and links from Connect.
3. **Duplicates** your shell into the next course — resources, TP points, criteria, tutors, time bands. Everything you built, none of the people.

Close-out will not run while a deferred candidate has no destination, an extension is outstanding, Cambridge has not confirmed grades, or an appeal window is open.

## The situations that go wrong

### A candidate withdraws

Formal and final; it needs a letter. They may still attend sessions unobserved.

- **Before the entry form is sent** to Cambridge, it is an internal event.
- **After**, they appear in the grades with the outcome **Withdrawn**.
- Their TP slot empties. Nobody else's position changes.
- Their file is **paused, not erased** — read-only, still in the assessor pack, because assessors do ask.

Withdrawn versus Fail is not a choice: a candidate who attends to the end and submits a portfolio, even an incomplete one, is a **Fail**. One who does not attend the final day and has not signed the CELTA 5 declaration is **Withdrawn**.

### A candidate defers

Only if they have completed **more than half the course**. Otherwise it is a withdrawal — though you may let them restart from the beginning without a new fee, carrying any successful assessment.

Everything freezes exactly as it stands, including work mid-marking, and transfers to the new course as their portfolio. How many hours carry across is **your judgement, not a calculation** — Cambridge asks you to weigh how much was completed, how they performed, and what the break will do.

The new course should be the **same mode of delivery** unless the candidate agrees otherwise in writing. Re-integration normally within six months.

### A tutor leaves

Deactivated, never deleted. Everything they wrote or signed keeps their name permanently. Their groups, marking and channels transfer to a named successor from that point.

A replacement **may not mark a lesson they did not watch**. They observe the next one themselves.

### Suspected plagiarism

A scanner runs on every submission, comparing against this course, your archive of previous cohorts, the brief and any model answers. It shows the matched passage and its length — never a percentage, because a number invites a threshold and thresholds decide cases they should not.

Findings appear **on the assignment when a tutor opens it to mark**. Nothing appears on a roster, in an email, or anywhere a candidate could see.

A concern is not a case. Most are a shared textbook definition. When it is a case:

1. Marking pauses. No outcome is recorded.
2. It is put to the candidate **in person**, not by a status changing overnight.
3. Their account is recorded, in their words. **A case cannot be decided without it.**
4. The decision comes from **your own malpractice policy** — Connect never invents a penalty.

Every case, upheld or not, goes in the assessor pack. A not-upheld case is evidence that you look properly.

### A candidate queries their grade

Answer it properly and most of these end there. Connect assembles the evidence: what both grade descriptors require, every TP outcome, which criteria were not met, assignment rounds, and the dates of tutorials and letters.

You write two things it cannot: what would have made the difference, and what happens next.

It quotes the record rather than re-arguing the grade. And if assembling it shows the grade was wrong, say so and correct it — the document exists to make the decision checkable, which means being willing to lose.

**Formal appeals** come after your own complaints procedure and after Cambridge confirms the result. Two-week deadlines apply at both ends.

## The five things that matter

1. **Lock the timetable before anything else.** Rotation, TP points, deadlines and every candidate's home screen read from it.
2. **The entry form date changes what a withdrawal means.** Record when it was sent.
3. **A slash in a provisional grade means you owe a justification** before you can finalise.
4. **Warnings must be issued with two lessons left**, not at the end.
5. **Your course lives on your Drive, not here.** Close-out is a delivery, not a deletion.

## Where the app teaches you

Connect does not have a manual inside it, and that is deliberate — a product that needs one has failed. Instead:

- **Empty screens explain themselves.** The first time you open Rotation it explains halves of three. Once there is content, the explanation goes and never returns.
- **Blocked buttons say why.** "Lock the timetable before assigning rounds" teaches the model better than any tour.
- **New ideas get one note, once.** Dismissed permanently.

If something is unclear and none of those helped, that is a design failure worth reporting rather than a gap in your reading.
