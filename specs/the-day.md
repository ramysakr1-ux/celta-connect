# The day, and the course hours

Settled 9 August. Supersedes the working-paper figures where they differ.

**6.25 contact hours a day.**

## What counts, and what does not

The handbook asks for "at least 120 contact hours between the candidates and course tutors", and names what counts: input · tutorial support and consultation · supervised lesson planning · six hours' assessed teaching practice per candidate · feedback on teaching practice · peer observation · six hours' directed observation, up to three of which may be filmed.

**Lunch and breaks do not count.** Neither does unsupervised work — that belongs to the separate 80 hours for reading, assignment writing and lesson preparation.

**Consultation does count.** A tutor in the room from 17:15 while candidates finish plans and ask questions is contact time, named in the handbook in its own right. It stops counting only if they are left alone — so that slot must be staffed, and it is not go-home time.

**Peer observation counts**, even though the candidate is watching rather than teaching. So does filmed observation.

---

## Input sessions are 45 minutes, two a day

**Two 45s rather than one 90, and the reason is the staging.** A single 90-minute phonology session opens every phonological sub-criterion at once. Split into three, as the centre's canon does, and 4i·4 opens after stress while 2e·3 waits for sounds — the criterion comes into scope the day it is actually taught rather than the day its general area was covered.

The same applies to gates: assignment 2 waits for vocabulary *and* function language rather than for one broad lexis session.

**90 minutes stays available by pairing two consecutive slots** — for language analysis, or a framework session with practice built in. Forty-five is the unit; ninety is a deliberate choice.

Twenty-seven core sessions at 45 minutes is 20.25 hours of input, comfortably inside the 125.

## Corrections to earlier figures

**The 110-hour figure was wrong.** It counted only tutor-led blocks and dropped the consultation slot, which does count.

**The 145-hour figure was also wrong.** It counted the whole clock day including lunch, which does not count.

**"No float" was wrong.** Four weeks at 125 has margin — enough for early finishes on marking days, observation in the tail, and a lost day. The binding constraint on the four-week course is the assignment and resubmission chain, not the hours.

**Deadlines stay at 09:00.** The proposal to move them to 18:00 assumed candidates write assignments during the course day. They do not: mornings are teaching and peer observation, afternoons are input and supervised planning. A candidate writing while their peers teach has stopped observing, which defeats the point of being in the room. Assignment writing happens in the evening whatever the deadline is, so the deadline sits where it disrupts the course least.

---

## The tail is the flexible part

Monday is finishing plans and the two consultation days are fixed by which tutor taught the second input. **Wednesday and Friday move.**

- **Week 1** — filmed observations 1 and 2. Models are worth most while candidates are still forming what a lesson looks like, and worth nothing in week five.
- **Week 2** — filmed observation 3, then Focus on the learner at the end of the week, once candidates have taught four times and know a learner rather than a name.
- **Week 3** — resubmission clinic, after assignment 1 has come back and the resubmissions are known. It is for the candidates holding one: they have the unmet criteria in writing and specific questions about them. Before that every question is "how long should it be".
- **Weeks 4–5** — open. This is where early finishes live.

**Consultation is the most movable thing on the course**, and that is fine. Stage 2 tutorials take most of an afternoon — an input slot plus the tail, twice in the week they fall — and displace whatever was there. Stage 1 is not universal: where one is needed it takes a tail slot. Filmed observations move the same way. None of it is fixed, and none of it needs to be.

**On the Scrivener DVD:** it carries one complete one-hour lesson and a set of short technique demonstrations. The hour does not fit a 45-minute tail slot, so it runs across two consecutive days with a task at the halfway point, or takes an input slot alongside the tail. Only the full lesson counts toward the six directed-observation hours; technique clips are input.
