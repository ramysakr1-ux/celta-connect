# The remaining compliance items

Five requirements from `Compliance Audit.dc.html` that need building but not designing. Each is a field, a band or a shelf — no new screen. Written for the developer; the audit file holds the handbook clause and the quoted wording for each.

---

## 1 · Two of the four assignments must be in academic prose

**Syllabus, assessment requirements** — "two of the assignments must be written in academic prose".

Add a `format` field to each assignment brief: `prose` or `structured`. The centre's own briefs already prescribe sections and tables; this only records which two are continuous prose.

Validate when a centre edits its brief set: exactly two must be `prose`. Warn rather than refuse — a centre mid-edit should not be blocked — but the course cannot be published with the wrong count.

Default on the shipped briefs: **Focus on the Learner** and **Lessons from the Classroom** are prose; **Language Related Tasks** and **Language Skills Related Tasks** are structured.

---

## 2 · Consultation time is a named band in the timetable

**Syllabus, course programmes** — the 120 hours are listed as input, lesson planning, teaching practice, feedback on teaching, peer observation, observation of experienced teachers, and **consultation time**.

The reference timetable has tutorials and self-study but no band called consultation. Add it as a band type, so the hours breakdown a centre shows an assessor uses the syllabus's own vocabulary rather than the centre's.

Nothing else changes: consultation slots already exist in practice (tutorials, resubmission clinics, planning support). They are being renamed into a category, not invented.

The 120-hour total must break down into exactly the syllabus's named categories, with no "other".

---

## 3 · Asynchronous input requires a linked live session

**Handbook 3.4** — recorded or Moodle input "must always be combined with opportunities for live follow-up discussion".

An input session card already holds slides, handouts, recordings and links. Add a flag: is this session **delivered** asynchronously, or does it merely have a recording of a live session?

- A live session with a recording attached: no requirement.
- An asynchronous session: requires a **linked live follow-up slot** on the timetable before the course can be published.

The link is to a timetable slot, not free text — otherwise it becomes a box somebody types "in class" into.

Refuse publication rather than warn. This is one of the rules an assessor checks directly on a course with any online element.

---

## 4 · The electronic candidate information sheet

**CELTA 5, front matter** — candidates are sent to complete one before the course.

Today it is a separate form outside the app, which means the data exists twice and the CELTA 5 header is typed by hand.

Fold its fields into the enrolment forms already designed, and **populate the CELTA 5 header from them**. Nobody should type a candidate's name into a CELTA 5.

Include the **Unique Learner Number** where a UK centre needs one — a field that is hidden unless the centre is in the UK.

---

## 5 · A Cambridge documents shelf

**CELTA 5, centre responsibilities** — the centre must ensure the CELTA Syllabus and the Administration Handbook are available on request, and must display the Centre Authorisation Certificate.

Add a shelf at **organisation level**, above centre and course, holding Cambridge's own documents:

- CELTA Syllabus
- CELTA Administration Handbook
- Centre Authorisation Certificate (per centre — approval is per centre, not per organisation)
- Cambridge Appeals Procedure

One copy, read by every course. Visible to candidates, tutors, admins and the assessor alike — these are the documents everyone is entitled to see.

This is the only category currently missing from the resource hub, and it is the cheapest of the five.

---

## Not being built

Two items from the audit are deliberately **not** enforced:

**The eight-student class average** (9.1.3). Derive it from the register and show it as a live figure. Do not warn and do not block — a centre cannot make volunteers turn up, and the honest answer to an assessor is the register itself.

**Assessor reuse limits** (13.3). A centre does not choose its assessor and repeat visits are common. Keep the assessor history per centre so it can be seen; do not flag it.

## Changed by decision

**Quiet hours derive from the timetable.** The centre's netiquette guidance says not to contact tutors late at night, but a part-time course teaches until 21:30. Messages are ordinary until an hour after the last timetabled session that day, and held until morning after that. A full-time course goes quiet in the early evening; a part-time one does not go quiet until half past ten.

**Candidates raise concerns with the assessor in the meeting**, not through a written channel. The assessor has no chat access, a form invites a complaints box nobody asked for, and the meeting is already on the timetable. What candidates get instead is an announcement two days before, written to calm rather than warn.
